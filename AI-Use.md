# AI Use and Evidence

AI assistance is allowed and encouraged as an engineering aid. It does not replace observation, testing, or understanding.

## Permitted uses

You may ask an AI assistant to explain concepts; propose configurations, queries, parsers, dashboards, tests, or scripts; help interpret an evidenced error; critique a hypothesis or experiment; and review documentation.

## Required verification

For every material AI contribution, record:

| Field | Required record |
|---|---|
| Goal | The problem being solved |
| Prompt | Exact prompt or exported-conversation link |
| Proposed output | Configuration, query, code, or advice |
| Risks/assumptions | What could make it wrong or unsafe |
| Verification | Tests, documentation, logs, or packet evidence |
| Changes | What the team changed and why |
| Owner/reviewer | Responsible student and peer reviewer |

Store entries in `ai/AI-ledger.md` and redact secrets.

## Learning checks

Each student must select one materially used AI-assisted artifact and explain it without the conversation, identify a flaw in an early response, show the test that bounded it, modify it for a new requirement, and explain what would justify production use.

An artifact that works but cannot be explained receives limited credit. Fabricated AI logs or tests violate academic integrity.

## Prohibited uses

- Sending credentials, keys, tokens, or sensitive logs to an external AI service.
- Asking an AI system to attack targets outside this lab.
- Submitting generated work without testing and attribution.
- Treating AI-generated claims as experimental evidence.
- Allowing an AI agent to change gateway controls without a reviewed diff and rollback.
